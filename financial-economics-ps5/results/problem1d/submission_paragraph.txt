I simulated 100,000 annual observations using the stated calibration and one shared IID standard-normal shock per year (PCG64, seed 42). The sample standard deviations of log consumption growth, the log SDF, and the log equity return were 0.036133, 0.361335, and 0.036133, respectively, close to their population values of 0.036, 0.360, and 0.036. Their lag-one sample autocorrelations were all approximately 0.001298, consistent with zero population serial correlation. The riskfree rate was constant, with zero standard deviation and undefined autocorrelation. The consumption-growth/equity-return correlation was 1.000000, as implied by the model's constant price-dividend ratio. The results match the model's theoretical moments but fail to match important empirical features discussed in the course: consumption growth is only moderately correlated with stock returns (Lecture 4, printed p. 146), and riskfree rates exhibit nonzero volatility (Lecture 4, printed p. 156). The model's ordinary equity-return SD is 4.13%, versus 16.54% for annual real S&P returns in 1889-1978 (Mehra and Prescott, 1985, Table 1). The model's 3.6% consumption-growth volatility is a calibration input. This exercise does not estimate new moments from an empirical dataset.
